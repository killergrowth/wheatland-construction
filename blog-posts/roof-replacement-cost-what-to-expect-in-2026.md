## What Does a Roof Replacement Actually Cost in 2026?

If your roof is showing its age — missing shingles, persistent leaks, or that telltale sagging in the middle — you're probably wondering what you're in for financially. Roof replacement cost is one of the most searched questions for Wichita homeowners, and for good reason. It's not a cheap project, but it's also not a mystery. Here's a straight-ahead breakdown of what to expect.

**National average:** $9,000–$12,000 for a standard asphalt shingle roof
**Wichita, KS range:** $7,500–$18,000+ depending on size, materials, and complexity

That's a wide range — so let's talk about what actually moves the number.

--

## Key Factors That Affect Roof Replacement Cost

### 1. Roof Size and Pitch

Roofing is priced by the "square" — 100 square feet of roof surface. A typical Wichita ranch home might have 20–28 squares. Steeper pitches require more safety equipment and labor time, which adds to the total.

### 2. Material Choice

This is the single biggest cost lever:

- **Asphalt shingles (3-tab):** $4–$6 per sq ft installed — most common, budget-friendly

- **Architectural shingles:** $5–$8 per sq ft — better durability, more curb appeal

- **Metal roofing:** $10–$18 per sq ft — long lifespan, great for Kansas hail

- **Tile or slate:** $15–$30+ per sq ft — premium look, significant weight load

For most Wichita homes, architectural asphalt shingles hit the best balance of cost and longevity.

### 3. Tear-Off vs. Overlay

If your existing roof can support a second layer, some contractors will overlay new shingles on top of old ones — cheaper upfront, but it masks underlying issues and can void warranties. A full tear-off is almost always the right call, especially on older Wichita homes that have already seen a Kansas hailstorm or two.

### 4. Decking Repairs

Once the old shingles come off, damaged or rotted decking gets exposed. In older homes — or post-hail — replacing sections of plywood decking adds $2–$4 per sq ft to the job. This isn't padding; it's a necessary step to avoid future problems.

### 5. Ventilation, Flashing, and Accessories

Proper ridge vents, drip edge, ice-and-water shield, and flashing around chimneys and vents are non-negotiables for a roof that lasts. Cut-rate bids often skip or cheap out on these. Ask every contractor what's included.

--

## Wichita-Specific Considerations

Wichita sits in a corridor of Kansas known for severe hail, high winds, and dramatic temperature swings. That matters for roof replacement decisions:

- **Hail-resistant shingles (Class 4 impact rating)** can lower your homeowner's insurance premium and are worth the small upcharge in this part of Kansas

- **Insurance claims** — if recent storm damage is part of why you're replacing the roof, document everything before tear-off begins. Your contractor should be willing to work directly with your adjuster

- **Permit requirements** — Wichita requires permits for full roof replacements. Any contractor skipping this step is a red flag

--

## What a Typical Wichita Roof Replacement Includes

A legitimate bid should itemize:

- Complete removal and disposal of existing shingles

- Inspection and repair of decking

- Ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys

- Felt underlayment across the full deck

- New shingles (manufacturer and product name)

- Ridge cap, drip edge, and step flashing

- Ventilation assessment and upgrades if needed

- Cleanup and haul-away

If a bid doesn't break these out, ask why.

--

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **Storm chasers** — out-of-state crews that flood Wichita after hail events, take deposits, and disappear

- **Unusually low bids** — almost always mean skipped steps, inferior materials, or unlicensed labor

- **Cash-only, no contract** — not a deal, it's a liability

- **No local references** — a Wichita roofer should be able to point to neighborhoods where you can see their work
