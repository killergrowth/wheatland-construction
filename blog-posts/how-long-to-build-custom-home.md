## The Honest Answer: It Depends — But Here's the Real Timeline

If you've started Googling "how long does it take to build a custom home," you've probably already seen answers ranging from 7 months to 2 years. That's a wide window, and it doesn't help much when you're trying to plan your life around a move-in date.

Here in Wichita, KS, we've built enough custom homes to give you a straight answer with real context. The average custom home build takes **12 to 18 months from contract to move-in** — but several factors push that number in either direction. Let's break it down phase by phase.

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Construction (1–4 Months)

Before a single nail gets driven, there's a lot of groundwork — literally and figuratively.

- **Design and architecture:** Finalizing your floor plan, elevations, and custom features can take 4–8 weeks depending on how many revisions are involved.
- **Permitting:** In Wichita and surrounding Sedgwick County areas, permit approval typically runs 2–6 weeks.
- **Site prep and lot work:** If you're building on raw land, clearing, grading, and utility rough-in add time up front.

The biggest delays in this phase? Indecision on finishes and slow permit turnarounds. Come in with a clear vision and a builder who knows the local approval process, and you'll move faster.

---

## Phase 2: Foundation (2–4 Weeks)

Once permits are pulled and the lot is ready, foundation work begins. In Kansas, most custom homes use poured concrete slab or full basements. Weather matters here — a wet spring in south-central Kansas can push foundation pours back by weeks.

---

## Phase 3: Framing, Rough-In, and Exterior (2–4 Months)

This is where your home starts to take shape. Framing, roofing, windows, and doors go in first. Then mechanical trades — plumbing, electrical, and HVAC — run their rough-ins before walls close.

This phase is heavily dependent on subcontractor scheduling. An experienced general contractor with established trade relationships in the Wichita area keeps this moving. Builders who are juggling too many projects or working with unfamiliar subs often stall here.

---

## Phase 4: Insulation, Drywall, and Interior Rough Work (4–8 Weeks)

After inspections clear the rough-in work, insulation goes in and drywall starts. This phase tends to move steadily — it's more labor-intensive than complex. Expect 4–8 weeks for a typical custom home in the 2,000–3,500 sq ft range.

---

## Phase 5: Finishes (2–4 Months)

Cabinets, flooring, tile, countertops, trim, paint, lighting, plumbing fixtures — this is where your selections come to life, and where timelines can slip badly if materials aren't ordered ahead of time.

**Pro tip:** Work with a builder who gets your finish selections locked in during pre-construction. Lead times on custom cabinetry and specialty tile can run 8–12 weeks. Order late and your build stalls.

---

## Phase 6: Final Inspections and Punch List (2–4 Weeks)

Before you get keys, the city of Wichita requires a final inspection sign-off. Your builder will also walk through a punch list — a checklist of items to touch up or complete before closing. A good builder wraps this efficiently. A disorganized one drags it out.

---

## What Makes Wichita Custom Home Builds Unique?

Building in Wichita has some distinct advantages. Land costs are lower than most metros, permitting is manageable compared to larger cities, and there's a strong local trade community. But Kansas weather — especially spring storms, high winds, and freeze-thaw cycles — can interrupt outdoor work phases. Building with a contractor who plans around the local season calendar keeps your project on schedule.

---

## The Bottom Line

Here's a realistic snapshot for a standard custom home build in the Wichita, KS area:

| Phase | Estimated Time |
|---|---|
| Pre-Construction | 1–4 months |
| Foundation | 2–4 weeks |
| Framing & Rough-In | 2–4 months |
| Drywall & Interior | 4–8 weeks |
| Finishes | 2–4 months |
| Final Inspections | 2–4 weeks |
| **Total** | **10–18 months** |

The biggest variable isn't the weather or the trades — it's the builder. A contractor who communicates clearly, schedules proactively, and has strong relationships in the local market will consistently outperform one who doesn't.
