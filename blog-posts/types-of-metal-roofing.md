## What Is Metal Roofing — and Why Are Wichita Homeowners Switching?

Metal roofing has gone from an industrial staple to one of the fastest-growing residential roofing choices in Wichita, KS. With Kansas weather throwing everything from hailstorms to scorching summers to ice storms at your roof, durability isn't optional — it's essential. Metal delivers it.

But "metal roofing" isn't one thing. There are several distinct types, each with different looks, price points, performance characteristics, and installation requirements. Before you commit, it's worth knowing what you're choosing between.

This guide breaks down every major type of metal roofing so you can make a confident, informed decision.

---

## The Main Types of Metal Roofing

### 1. Standing Seam Metal Roofing

Standing seam is the gold standard of residential metal roofing. It features continuous panels that run vertically from the ridge to the eave, with raised interlocking seams that hide all fasteners from view.

**Why homeowners love it:**
- No exposed fasteners means fewer leak points over time
- Sleek, modern look that works on a wide range of architectural styles
- Exceptional wind and hail resistance — critical for Wichita's storm season
- Lifespan of 40–70 years with minimal maintenance

**Trade-off:** It's the most expensive metal roofing option upfront, but the long-term ROI is hard to beat.

---

### 2. Metal Shingles and Shakes

Metal shingles are stamped or formed to mimic the look of traditional asphalt shingles, wood shakes, or even slate — without the weight or maintenance headaches. If you want the curb appeal of a classic roof with the durability of metal, this is your option.

**Why homeowners love it:**
- Looks familiar; easy to match existing neighborhood aesthetic
- Lighter than clay or slate alternatives
- Strong hail and fire ratings
- Available in a wide range of colors and finishes

**Trade-off:** Slightly more installation complexity than standard asphalt. Not all contractors are experienced with them — make sure yours is.

---

### 3. Corrugated Metal Roofing

Corrugated panels are the wavy or ribbed metal sheets most people picture when they think "metal roof." Historically used on barns and agricultural buildings, corrugated metal has made a comeback in modern farmhouse and industrial-style residential design.

**Why homeowners love it:**
- Most affordable metal roofing option
- Lightweight and easy to install
- Great for outbuildings, garages, and additions
- Durable and effective at shedding water

**Trade-off:** Exposed fasteners are standard, which means more potential maintenance points over the years. Not typically the best fit for high-end residential projects.

---

### 4. Metal Tile Roofing

Metal tile profiles are designed to replicate the look of traditional clay or concrete tile — think Mediterranean or Spanish-style rooflines — at a fraction of the weight and maintenance burden. For Wichita homes with that aesthetic, metal tile delivers the look without the structural reinforcement usually required for real clay.

**Why homeowners love it:**
- Distinctive, upscale appearance
- Much lighter than real clay or concrete tile
- Good energy efficiency due to airflow design
- Wind and impact resistant

**Trade-off:** Higher cost than corrugated or basic metal shingles. Color options can be more limited depending on the manufacturer.

---

### 5. Ribbed or Exposed Fastener Metal Panels

Ribbed panels — sometimes called R-panels or 5-V crimp — are a step up from corrugated in terms of profile and versatility. They're commonly used on commercial buildings but have found a home in residential construction, especially for garages, shops, and modern-style homes.

**Why homeowners love it:**
- Cost-effective with a clean, structured look
- Fast installation
- Works well on low-slope roofs
- Widely available from local suppliers in the Wichita area

**Trade-off:** Like corrugated, exposed fasteners require periodic inspection and re-sealing over time.

## Metal Roofing Materials: What's the Roof Actually Made Of?

The profile (shape) is just one decision. The metal itself matters too:

- **Steel (Galvalume or Galvanized):** Most common, most affordable. Galvalume adds an aluminum-zinc coating for superior corrosion resistance — a smart choice in Kansas where humidity and temperature swings accelerate rust.
- **Aluminum:** Naturally rust-resistant, great for coastal areas but also performs well in Kansas. Slightly softer than steel, so more susceptible to hail denting.
- **Copper:** Premium, long-lasting, develops a patina over time. Beautiful but expensive.
- **Zinc:** European-favored, self-healing finish, very long lifespan. Still emerging in the U.S. market.

For most Wichita homeowners, **Galvalume steel** hits the sweet spot of performance and value.
