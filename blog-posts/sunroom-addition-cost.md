## Sunroom Addition Cost: What to Expect in 2026

If you've been thinking about adding a sunroom to your Wichita home, you're not alone. Sunrooms are one of the most popular home additions in Kansas right now—and for good reason. They add usable square footage, boost natural light, and increase your home's value without the full cost of a traditional room addition.

But what does a sunroom actually cost in 2026? That's the question most homeowners ask first, and it deserves a straight answer.

### Average Sunroom Addition Cost in 2026

Nationally, sunroom additions range from **$15,000 to $75,000+**, depending on size, materials, and how "finished" the space needs to be. Here in Wichita, KS, you can generally expect:

- **Three-season sunroom:** $15,000–$30,000
- **Four-season sunroom (insulated, climate-controlled):** $30,000–$60,000
- **Premium glass room or fully custom build:** $60,000–$80,000+

These ranges assume a typical 150–300 sq ft addition. Larger footprints or complex rooflines push costs higher.

### What Drives the Price Up (or Down)

Sunroom cost isn't one-size-fits-all. Here are the biggest factors that move the needle:

**1. Three-Season vs. Four-Season**
A three-season sunroom uses basic framing, single-pane glass, and no HVAC—it's usable spring through fall in most of Kansas. A four-season room is fully insulated, connected to your home's heating and cooling system, and built to the same standard as interior living space. That distinction alone can double the price.

**2. Foundation Type**
Some sunrooms sit on an existing patio slab. Others require a new poured concrete foundation. If your project needs a new foundation, budget an extra $3,000–$8,000 depending on soil conditions and size.

**3. Glass Package**
Standard single-pane glass is cheaper but performs poorly in Wichita winters. Low-E double-pane glass costs more upfront but pays off in energy bills and comfort year-round.

**4. Permits and Site Conditions**
Wichita requires building permits for permanent additions. Permit fees are typically $500–$1,500 for a project this size. Unusual site conditions—drainage issues, utility conflicts, steep grades—add to the overall cost.

**5. Interior Finishes**
A basic sunroom might have a concrete floor and drywall. A finished sunroom could include tile or hardwood flooring, ceiling fans, electrical outlets, custom blinds, or even a mini-split system. Each upgrade adds cost but also adds livability.

### Labor Costs: What to Expect in Wichita

Labor is typically 40–50% of a sunroom project's total cost. In the Wichita market, general contractors building sunroom additions usually charge between **$50–$90 per hour** for skilled labor, with specialty trades (electricians, HVAC techs) billing separately at similar or higher rates.

Avoid the temptation to hire the cheapest bid. Sunroom additions involve structural work, weatherproofing, and tie-ins to your existing home's framing—cutting corners here creates problems that cost far more to fix later.

### Is a Sunroom Worth It in 2026?

In most cases, yes. According to national remodeling data, sunrooms return **50–70% of their cost** at resale—and in a market like Wichita where livable outdoor-adjacent space is in demand, a well-built sunroom can be a strong selling point.

More importantly, the day-to-day value is real. A sunroom gives you a place to drink your morning coffee in February without freezing, grow plants year-round, or just have a bright room that doesn't feel like the rest of the house.
