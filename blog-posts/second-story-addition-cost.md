## Second Story Addition Cost: What to Expect in 2026

Adding a second story to your home is one of the biggest investments a Wichita homeowner can make — and one of the smartest. Instead of buying a bigger house in a competitive market, you expand the one you already love. But before you break ground, you need a clear picture of what it actually costs.

Here's the straight talk on second story addition cost in 2026, with real numbers and the factors that move the price.

---

## What Does a Second Story Addition Cost in 2026?

Nationally, second story additions range from **$100 to $300+ per square foot**, putting a full second-floor addition somewhere between **$150,000 and $600,000** depending on size and scope.

In Wichita, KS, you'll generally see costs on the lower end of that national range — labor costs here are more reasonable than coastal markets — but materials, permitting, and engineering fees have climbed significantly since 2023. A realistic budget for a full second story addition in Wichita runs **$120,000 to $350,000** for most homes.

Here's a quick breakdown by project size:

| Project Scope | Estimated Cost (Wichita) |
|---|---|
| Partial addition (1–2 rooms) | $80,000 – $150,000 |
| Full second story (1,200–1,800 sq ft) | $150,000 – $300,000 |
| Full addition + full finish-out | $250,000 – $400,000+ |

These are ballpark figures. Your actual number depends on several variables.

---

## What Drives the Cost of a Second Story Addition?

### 1. Foundation and Structural Work
Before a single piece of lumber goes up, an engineer has to confirm your existing foundation and first-floor framing can carry the load. If they can't, you're looking at reinforcement work — which adds cost. This is the most common budget surprise homeowners face.

### 2. Roofline Complexity
A simple gable roof costs less. Complex hip roofs, dormers, or mixed rooflines drive labor and material costs up fast. The more cuts and angles involved, the more time on the clock.

### 3. Square Footage
More square footage means more framing, more roofing, more drywall, more flooring, more electrical, more HVAC. It scales pretty directly. Know your target size before you get quotes.

### 4. Interior Finish Level
A basic finish — standard fixtures, builder-grade flooring, standard trim — keeps costs down. High-end finishes (tile showers, hardwood floors, custom millwork) can add $30,000–$80,000 on their own.

### 5. HVAC Expansion
Your existing HVAC system almost certainly isn't sized for a new floor. Expect to either upgrade your system or add a separate mini-split setup for the new space. Budget $8,000–$20,000 depending on your current system.

### 6. Permitting and Engineering (Wichita-Specific)
Wichita requires permits for structural additions, and you'll need stamped engineered drawings. Permit and engineering fees typically run **$3,000–$8,000** on a project of this size. Factor it in from day one.

## Second Story Addition vs. Buying a New Home

At first glance, moving seems easier. But in Wichita's current market, buying a larger home means:

- Higher sale price per square foot
- Higher interest rate on a new mortgage
- Transaction costs (agent fees, closing costs, moving)
- Leaving a neighborhood and lot you already like

A second story addition lets you stay put, build equity in your current home, and get exactly the layout you want — not whatever's available on the MLS.

## How Long Does It Take?

A full second story addition in Wichita typically runs **4 to 7 months** from permit approval to move-in. That includes:

- Engineering and design: 4–8 weeks
- Permit approval: 3–6 weeks
- Construction: 10–18 weeks
- Punch list and final inspections: 2–4 weeks

Plan for weather delays in Kansas — spring storms can push timelines, especially during roof framing.
