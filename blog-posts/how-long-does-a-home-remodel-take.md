## How Long Does a Home Remodel Take? Here's What You Need to Know

If you're planning a major remodel in Wichita, KS, one of the first questions you'll ask is: *how long is this going to take?* It's a fair question — and an important one. Whether you're gutting a kitchen, adding a room, or doing a full home renovation, the timeline affects everything from your budget to where you're sleeping at night.

The honest answer? It depends. But that doesn't mean you have to go in blind. Here's a realistic breakdown of what to expect.

---

## Why Remodel Timelines Vary So Much

No two remodels are identical. A few factors that directly impact how long your project takes:

- **Scope of work** — A bathroom update takes weeks; a whole-home remodel can take months.
- **Permitting** — In Wichita, permits are required for most structural, electrical, and plumbing work. Processing time varies.
- **Material lead times** — Custom cabinets, windows, and specialty finishes can take 6–12 weeks to arrive.
- **Contractor availability** — A reputable contractor books out. Plan ahead.
- **Unexpected discoveries** — Old homes in Wichita often hide surprises: outdated wiring, plumbing issues, or structural quirks that need to be addressed before work can continue.

Understanding these variables upfront saves you frustration later.

---

## Realistic Timelines by Project Type

### Kitchen Remodel
**4 to 12 weeks**

A full kitchen gut — new layout, cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, and electrical — typically runs 8–12 weeks. A more straightforward refresh (keeping the layout, swapping surfaces) can wrap in 4–6 weeks. Countertop and cabinet lead times are often the bottleneck.

### Bathroom Remodel
**3 to 8 weeks**

A single bathroom update usually takes 3–5 weeks for a standard remodel. If you're expanding square footage, moving plumbing, or doing a full master suite overhaul, budget 6–8 weeks minimum.

### Room Addition
**3 to 6 months**

Adding square footage means foundation work, framing, roofline integration, insulation, drywall, mechanical rough-ins, and finish work. It also means more permits. This is one of the most complex project types — give yourself a full 3–6 months, sometimes longer depending on size and complexity.

### Basement Finish
**6 to 10 weeks**

Finishing an unfinished basement in a Wichita home — egress windows, framing, drywall, electrical, flooring — typically runs 6–10 weeks depending on the layout and whether you're adding a bathroom.

### Whole-Home Remodel
**4 to 12 months**

If you're touching every room, expect a multi-month project. A full gut-and-rebuild of an older Wichita home can run 6–12 months. Phased projects that allow you to stay in the home take longer than vacant-home builds.

## What Can Slow a Remodel Down?

Even the best-planned projects hit speed bumps. The most common causes of delays:

- **Permit hold-ups** — City inspections are required at multiple stages. Scheduling inspections and waiting for approval adds time.
- **Change orders** — Scope creep is real. Every "while you're at it" request adds time and cost.
- **Weather** — Exterior work and concrete pours are weather-dependent. Wichita winters can pause outdoor phases.
- **Material backorders** — Specific finishes or fixtures may have long lead times. Order early.
- **Labor sequencing** — Framing has to be done before drywall. Plumbing rough-in before insulation. Every trade has to move in the right order.

A good contractor will help you anticipate and plan around these issues — not just react to them.

---

## How to Set Realistic Expectations

**Start with a detailed scope.** The more clearly defined your project is before breaking ground, the more accurate your timeline will be.

**Build in buffer.** Whatever timeline you're quoted, mentally add 10–20% for unforeseen issues. It's not pessimism — it's planning.

**Communicate with your contractor.** Ask for a project schedule with milestones. Know when inspections are scheduled, when materials are arriving, and when each trade is on-site.

**Avoid mid-project changes.** Changing your mind after work has started is the single fastest way to blow a timeline.
