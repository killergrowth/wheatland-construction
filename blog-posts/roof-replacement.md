If you've lived in Wichita longer than a couple of hail seasons, you already know what our weather can do to a roof. Between the spring storms that roll through Sedgwick County and the summer heat that bakes shingles for months on end, roofs here work harder than almost anywhere else in the country. So when homeowners start asking "do I need a roof replacement, or can this one hang on a little longer?" — it's a fair question, and the answer isn't always obvious from the ground.

This guide walks through what actually goes into a roof replacement decision: the warning signs, the cost factors, the timeline, and what separates a contractor who does it right from one who's just chasing a hail claim.

## Signs Your Roof Is Telling You Something

Most roofs don't fail all at once. They give signals first, and knowing what to look for can save you from a surprise leak during the next big storm.

**On the outside**, look for curling or cupped shingles, bald spots where granules have worn away, cracked or missing shingles, and valleys where debris collects and water pools. If your roof is covered in dark streaks, that's typically algae — not necessarily a structural problem, but a sign the roof is aging.

**On the inside**, check your attic for daylight coming through the decking, water stains on the sheathing, or a musty smell that suggests trapped moisture. Ceiling stains downstairs are the most obvious red flag, but by the time you see one, water has usually been getting in for a while.

**After a storm**, hail bruising is the big one around here. Bruised shingles lose their granule protection and age faster, even if there's no visible hole. A [roof inspection](/basement-finishing/wichita-ks/) after any significant hail event is worth the 30 minutes it takes.

## How Long Should a Roof Actually Last?

Asphalt shingle roofs in Kansas typically run 20-25 years under normal conditions, but "normal" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Homes that have taken repeated hail hits, have poor attic ventilation, or sit under heavy tree cover often need replacement well before the 20-year mark. Metal roofing, by contrast, can push 40-50+ years with proper installation, which is part of why we're seeing more Wichita homeowners ask about it as an upgrade during a replacement.

Age alone isn't the deciding factor — condition is. A 12-year-old roof that took a bad hailstorm may need replacing sooner than an 18-year-old roof that's been protected and well-ventilated.

## What Drives the Cost of a Roof Replacement

Every roof is different, but a few factors consistently move the price:

- **Roof size and pitch** — more square footage and steeper slopes mean more labor and material.
- **Material choice** — standard architectural shingles sit at one price point; metal roofing costs more upfront but lasts significantly longer.
- **Decking condition** — if the plywood underneath has rotted or has water damage, that needs to be replaced before new shingles go down, which adds cost.
- **Number of layers being removed** — tearing off two old layers takes longer than one.
- **Access and complexity** — steep roofs, multiple valleys, chimneys, and skylights all add time.

The best way to get a real number is a physical inspection, not a phone estimate. Anyone quoting a full price without climbing on your roof is guessing.

## Insurance Claims and Roof Replacement

If your roof damage came from a hailstorm or wind event, your homeowner's insurance may cover some or all of the replacement, minus your deductible. This is where a lot of homeowners get taken advantage of — either by contractors who inflate claims or by insurance adjusters who lowball the scope of damage.

A contractor who's done this before in Wichita will know how to document hail bruising properly, meet the adjuster on-site, and make sure the final scope actually matches what the roof needs — not just what gets the claim closed fastest.

## Choosing the Right Contractor

A few things worth checking before you sign anything:

1. **Local presence** — a contractor with an actual Wichita address and a track record in this market is going to be around if something needs warranty service in year 3.
2. **Manufacturer certifications** — these often come with better warranty coverage on both materials and labor.
3. **Written scope of work** — get the tear-off, decking inspection, underlayment, and shingle brand spelled out in writing before work starts.
4. **References you can actually call** — not just a review page, but real neighbors or past clients.
