## The Roofing Decision Most Wichita Homeowners Overthink

When it's time to replace your roof, the debate between metal roofing and asphalt shingles comes up fast. Both are legitimate options — but they're built for different priorities, budgets, and long-term goals. If you're a Wichita homeowner trying to make the right call, this breakdown will cut through the noise.

Let's talk real numbers, real performance, and what actually makes sense for homes here in south-central Kansas.

---

## Cost: Upfront vs Long-Term

**Asphalt shingles** are the most budget-friendly option upfront. A standard 3-tab or architectural shingle roof typically runs **$4,000–$9,000** for an average-sized home, depending on pitch, square footage, and material grade.

**Metal roofing** costs more out of the gate — usually **$10,000–$20,000+** for the same home. Standing seam metal sits at the higher end; metal shingles or corrugated panels can be more affordable.

Here's the flip side: a quality metal roof can last **40–70 years**. Asphalt shingles average **15–25 years** in Kansas, where temperature swings, hail, and heavy wind are part of the package. If you're planning to stay in your home long-term, metal often wins the lifetime cost comparison.

---

## Durability in Kansas Weather

Wichita gets hit with everything — hail, 70 mph wind gusts, ice storms, and brutal summer heat. Your roof takes all of it.

**Asphalt shingles** hold up reasonably well but are more vulnerable to hail damage and high winds. After a significant storm, it's common to see granule loss, cracked tabs, or lifted edges on shingle roofs. That means more frequent insurance claims and repairs.

**Metal roofing** is significantly more impact-resistant. Most metal roofs carry a Class 4 impact rating — the highest available — which can also qualify you for **homeowner's insurance discounts** in Kansas. Metal doesn't crack, warp, or absorb moisture the way shingles can.

If storm durability is your top concern, metal has a clear edge here.

---

## Energy Efficiency

Kansas summers are no joke. Cooling costs spike from June through September, and your roof plays a real role in that.

Metal roofs reflect solar radiation rather than absorbing it, which can reduce cooling costs by **10–25%** compared to darker asphalt shingles. Many metal roofing systems are also compatible with spray foam insulation underneath, improving performance even further.

Architectural shingles in lighter colors have improved in this area, but metal still leads on reflectivity — especially with a quality coating.

---

## Aesthetics and Home Style

This one comes down to your home and your neighborhood.

**Asphalt shingles** offer a traditional look that blends easily into most residential areas. They come in hundreds of colors and textures, including options that mimic wood shake or slate.

**Metal roofing** has a more modern or industrial appearance — which some homeowners love and others don't. Standing seam metal especially reads as contemporary. If your home is a traditional craftsman or colonial style, you'll want to look at metal shingles or panels that complement the architecture.

Bottom line: don't let aesthetics be an afterthought. Your roof is a major visual element of your home's curb appeal.

---

## Maintenance

**Asphalt shingles** require periodic inspection, especially after storms. Granule loss, cracked tabs, and flashing issues are common over time and need attention before they become leaks.

**Metal roofing** is largely low-maintenance. You'll still want annual inspections and gutter cleaning, but there's no granule shedding, no curling edges, and no moss/algae growth like you sometimes see on shingles in shaded areas.

---

## Which One Is Right for You?

Here's the honest answer:

- **Choose shingles if** you're working with a tighter budget, planning to sell in the next 5–10 years, or want a traditional look that fits the neighborhood.
- **Choose metal if** you're staying long-term, want maximum storm resistance, value energy savings, or are tired of dealing with roof repairs every few years.

Both materials can be installed well or installed poorly. The contractor matters as much as the material.
