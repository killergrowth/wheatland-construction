## Home Renovation Checklist: Everything You Need to Know

Taking on a major remodel is one of the biggest investments you'll make as a homeowner. Whether you're gutting a kitchen, adding a room, or doing a full-home renovation in Wichita, KS, the difference between a smooth project and a costly nightmare usually comes down to one thing: preparation.

This home renovation checklist walks you through every phase — from planning to final walkthrough — so nothing falls through the cracks.

---

## Phase 1: Define Your Scope and Goals

Before anyone picks up a hammer, get clear on what you actually want.

- **List every room or area** you want to renovate
- **Prioritize by need vs. want** — structural issues always come first
- **Set a realistic timeline** — major remodels in Wichita typically take 3–6 months depending on scope
- **Identify your non-negotiables** — the things you won't compromise on, and the things you're flexible about

Being specific here saves you from expensive change orders down the road.

---

## Phase 2: Set Your Budget (Then Pad It)

Budget surprises are the #1 source of renovation stress. Build yours with a buffer.

- Get your total target number, then **add 15–20% as a contingency fund**
- Break costs into categories: **labor, materials, permits, design fees**
- Research material costs upfront — lumber, fixtures, and finishes vary widely
- Decide what you're financing vs. paying out of pocket
- Don't forget soft costs: temporary housing, storage units, eating out during a kitchen remodel

A good contractor will help you build a realistic budget based on local Wichita pricing before any work begins.

---

## Phase 3: Hire the Right Contractor

This step makes or breaks everything else.

- **Get at least 3 bids** — not just for price comparison, but to understand scope differences
- Verify licensing and insurance — every contractor working in Kansas should carry both
- **Check references and past project photos**
- Read contracts carefully — scope of work, payment schedule, timeline, and change order process should all be spelled out
- Avoid contractors who ask for large upfront payments or won't provide a written contract

For major remodels in the Wichita area, working with a local contractor who knows local codes and suppliers matters more than most people realize.

---

## Phase 4: Permits and Planning

Skipping permits is one of the most expensive mistakes homeowners make.

- Confirm which permits are required for your project (structural, electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
- Your contractor should pull permits — if they suggest skipping them, that's a red flag
- **Review Wichita's local building codes** or let your contractor handle this
- Finalize your design plans and material selections before work begins
- Order materials early — supply chain delays can push timelines by weeks

---

## Phase 5: During the Renovation

Once work starts, staying organized keeps the project on track.

- **Establish a single point of contact** with your contractor — daily or weekly check-ins depending on scope
- Document everything in writing — change orders, timeline adjustments, material substitutions
- Do a **weekly walkthrough** to catch issues early
- Keep a renovation journal or folder with all receipts, permits, and communications
- Protect areas of your home not being renovated — dust and debris travel

---

## Phase 6: Final Walkthrough and Punch List

Don't release final payment until this is done.

- Walk through every completed area with your contractor
- Create a **punch list** — a written list of anything incomplete or needing correction
- Test everything: outlets, plumbing, HVAC, windows, doors
- Confirm all permits have been closed and inspections passed
- Get copies of all warranties for materials and labor

---

## Common Renovation Mistakes to Avoid

- Underestimating timeline and budget
- Changing your mind mid-project (change orders cost more than upfront decisions)
- Hiring the cheapest bid without vetting the contractor
- Skipping the design phase and making decisions on the fly
- Not planning for where you'll live during major work
