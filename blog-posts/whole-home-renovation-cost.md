## What Does a Whole Home Renovation Actually Cost in 2026?

If you've been Googling "whole home renovation cost" and getting wildly different numbers — you're not alone. The range is genuinely wide. A full gut renovation on a 2,000 sq ft home can run anywhere from $100,000 to $400,000+ depending on scope, materials, and who you hire.

In Wichita, KS, homeowners tend to land somewhere in the middle of national averages, which works in your favor. Labor costs here are competitive compared to larger metro markets, and local contractors like Wheatland Construction know how to stretch your budget without cutting corners.

Here's a realistic breakdown of what you can expect.

## Average Whole Home Renovation Costs by Scope

Not every "whole home renovation" is the same. The scope you choose drives your final number more than almost anything else.

### Light Refresh — $30,000 to $75,000
This covers cosmetic updates: paint, flooring, fixtures, hardware, and maybe a kitchen facelift. No structural changes, no moving walls. If your home is in decent shape but just feels dated, this is often enough.

### Mid-Level Renovation — $75,000 to $175,000
This is where most Wichita homeowners land for a true whole home project. You're looking at new kitchens, updated bathrooms, HVAC or electrical work, and some layout changes. Quality finishes, real improvements — not just lipstick.

### Full Gut Renovation — $175,000 to $400,000+
Everything comes out. Down to the studs in many cases. New plumbing, new electrical, new layout, high-end finishes throughout. If you're buying a fixer-upper or doing a complete transformation, this is the path.

## What Drives the Cost Up (or Down)

Understanding cost drivers helps you make smarter decisions before a single nail gets pulled.

**Square footage** is the obvious one — more space means more materials, more labor, more time. But it's not always linear. Larger homes sometimes benefit from economy of scale on materials.

**Structural changes** get expensive fast. Moving a load-bearing wall, relocating plumbing stacks, or adding square footage all require engineering, permits, and specialty labor. In Wichita, permit costs and inspections add up but protect you legally and on resale.

**Material choices** can swing your budget by 30-50%. Quartz countertops vs. granite vs. laminate. Hardwood vs. LVP vs. tile. Custom cabinets vs. semi-custom vs. stock. Every room has these forks in the road.

**Hidden conditions** are the wildcard. Older Wichita homes — especially those built in the 50s, 60s, and 70s — sometimes reveal surprises: outdated wiring, knob-and-tube, galvanized pipes, moisture damage, or asbestos-containing materials. A good contractor will build a contingency into your estimate (typically 10-15%) for exactly this reason.

## What to Ask a Contractor Before You Sign Anything

Before you commit to a whole home renovation in Wichita, get clear answers on these:

- **Is your estimate fixed-price or cost-plus?** Fixed gives you predictability. Cost-plus means you share the risk of unknowns.
- **How do you handle change orders?** Every project has them. Know the process before work starts.
- **What does your warranty cover?** Materials only? Labor? For how long?
- **Are you pulling all required permits?** If a contractor says permits aren't necessary for significant work — walk away.
- **Who's on-site daily?** You want a consistent crew and a clear point of contact, not a revolving door.
