## Major Home Remodel Cost: What Wichita Homeowners Are Actually Paying in 2026

If you've started getting quotes for a major home remodel in Wichita, you already know the number that comes back can feel like a gut punch. Costs have shifted significantly over the past few years — material prices, labor availability, and project complexity all play a role. Before you sign anything or second-guess your budget, here's a straight-talk breakdown of what a major remodel actually costs in 2026, and what drives that number up or down.

---

## What Counts as a "Major" Home Remodel?

Not every renovation qualifies. A major home remodel typically involves structural changes, multiple rooms, or systems-level work — think:

- Full kitchen gut and rebuild
- Master suite additions or expansions
- Basement finishing or conversion
- Whole-home renovations
- Room additions with new foundation work

These projects go well beyond cosmetic updates. They involve permits, subcontractors, and coordination across trades — which is exactly why hiring the right general contractor matters.

---

## Average Major Home Remodel Costs in 2026

Here's what Wichita-area homeowners are seeing across common major remodel categories:

### Kitchen Remodel (Full Gut)
**$40,000 – $100,000+**
A mid-range full kitchen remodel in Wichita typically lands between $45,000–$65,000. High-end custom builds with new layout, appliances, and cabinetry push north of $80,000.

### Master Suite Addition
**$60,000 – $130,000**
Adding square footage to an existing home involves foundation work, framing, roofline tie-ins, plumbing, and electrical. Wichita's local labor costs keep this range more competitive than coastal markets, but material costs are national.

### Basement Finish
**$25,000 – $65,000**
Wichita homes with unfinished basements have a significant opportunity here. A full basement finish — framing, drywall, flooring, electrical, egress windows — typically runs $30,000–$50,000 depending on finish level.

### Whole-Home Renovation
**$100,000 – $300,000+**
Scope is everything here. Older Wichita homes (pre-1980) often surface surprises: outdated wiring, plumbing, insulation, or structural issues that add cost. A realistic whole-home renovation budget should include a 15–20% contingency.

---

## What Drives the Final Number

These are the variables that move your quote the most:

**1. Scope Creep**
Once walls open up, the unexpected happens. Older homes in neighborhoods like College Hill, Riverside, or Eastborough frequently have hidden issues — knob-and-tube wiring, original cast iron plumbing, or undersized HVAC systems.

**2. Material Selections**
The gap between builder-grade and mid-range materials can be $15,000–$30,000 on a single kitchen. Set your selections budget early.

**3. Permit Requirements**
Wichita requires permits for structural work, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. A reputable contractor pulls these for you — if someone offers to skip permits to save money, walk away.

**4. Labor Market**
Skilled trades in Kansas remain in high demand. Quality crews are booked weeks or months out. Rushing a timeline increases cost; planning ahead keeps it controlled.

**5. Seasonal Timing**
Late fall and winter tend to be slower seasons for interior remodels in Wichita. Starting a project in November vs. May can sometimes mean better crew availability and faster timelines.

---

## How to Budget Smart

- **Get 2–3 detailed bids** — not ballpark estimates. Real line-item quotes reveal what's included and what isn't.
- **Build in a contingency** — 10–15% minimum, 20% on older homes.
- **Prioritize by ROI** — kitchen and bathroom remodels consistently return the most value in the Wichita market.
- **Confirm financing early** — HELOCs, construction loans, and renovation financing all have different timelines and requirements.
