## What Does a Whole House Remodel Actually Cost in 2026?

If you're searching "whole house remodel cost," you're probably getting sticker shock from national averages that don't reflect what things actually cost in Wichita. So let's cut through the noise.

A whole house remodel in Wichita, KS typically runs between **$80,000 and $300,000+**, depending on the size of your home, the scope of work, and the finishes you choose. The national average hovers around $150,000—but Wichita's market tends to come in more favorably than coastal cities, which is one of the real advantages of renovating here.

---

## Breaking Down the Costs by Room

Understanding whole house remodel cost means looking at each space individually. Here's a realistic breakdown for a mid-size Wichita home:

### Kitchen Remodel
- **Budget:** $25,000–$50,000
- **Full gut/upscale:** $60,000–$100,000+
- Cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, and plumbing/electrical updates all factor in.

### Bathrooms
- **Primary bath:** $15,000–$40,000
- **Secondary/guest bath:** $8,000–$18,000
- Walk-in showers, tile work, vanities, and fixture upgrades drive costs up quickly.

### Living Areas & Bedrooms
- **Flooring, drywall, paint, trim:** $10,000–$30,000 depending on square footage
- Hardwood refinishing is more cost-effective than full replacement if the subfloor is solid.

### Structural & Systems
This is where whole house remodels can surprise homeowners. If the project touches HVAC, plumbing, electrical panels, or load-bearing walls, budget an additional **$20,000–$60,000** depending on the age and condition of your home.

## What Drives Whole House Remodel Cost Up (or Down)?

Several factors move the needle on your final number:

**Square footage** — More space means more materials and more labor hours. A 1,200 sq ft ranch and a 3,000 sq ft two-story are completely different projects.

**Age of the home** — Older homes in Wichita (pre-1980s especially) often uncover surprises: outdated wiring, galvanized plumbing, asbestos in flooring or insulation. Budget a 10–15% contingency for unknowns.

**Finish level** — Builder-grade finishes versus custom cabinetry, quartz countertops, and designer tile is often a $50,000+ difference in a whole house project.

**Permitting and code compliance** — Wichita requires permits for structural changes, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work. A reputable contractor handles this. If someone offers to skip permits, walk away.

**Timeline** — Faster timelines often mean premium labor costs. A realistic whole house remodel takes **4–9 months** depending on scope.

## How to Budget for a Major Remodel Without Getting Burned

1. **Get at least three bids** — and make sure they're scoped identically so you're comparing apples to apples.
2. **Build in a 15–20% contingency** — especially in an older home. Something almost always comes up.
3. **Prioritize structural and systems first** — kitchens and baths get the attention, but a home with good bones, solid electrical, and efficient HVAC will serve you better long-term.
4. **Clarify what's included** — permits, demo, cleanup, and material procurement should all be spelled out in writing.
5. **Don't value-engineer the contractor** — the cheapest bid usually costs more in the long run through delays, callbacks, or substandard work.
