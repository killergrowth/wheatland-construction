## What to Expect When Building a Custom Home in Wichita, KS

Building a custom home is one of the biggest investments you'll ever make — and one of the most rewarding. But if you've never been through the process before, it can feel overwhelming. Timelines slip. Decisions pile up. Costs shift. And suddenly you're wondering if you should have just bought something off the market.

The good news: most of that stress comes from not knowing what's coming. When you understand the process from lot to move-in, you can make better decisions, avoid common mistakes, and actually enjoy the build.

Here's what to expect when building a custom home in Wichita — start to finish.

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Construction Planning (2–4 Months)

Before a single nail goes in, there's a lot of groundwork to cover.

**Find Your Lot**
In the Wichita area, you have options — established neighborhoods in Andover or Derby, new developments in Goddard, or rural land further out. Your lot affects your design, utilities, drainage, and cost more than most people realize. Get a soil test and check for easements before you commit.

**Set Your Budget — Then Add a Buffer**
A realistic custom home budget in the Wichita market right now typically ranges from $150–$300+ per square foot depending on finishes, lot work, and complexity. Set your number, then build in a 10–15% contingency. Things come up. They always do.

**Choose a Builder and Finalize Your Plans**
This is the most important decision you'll make. Interview at least two or three builders. Ask for references. Look at past work. A good Wichita builder will walk you through their process transparently and help you understand what you're getting into before you sign anything.

Once you've selected a builder, you'll work through architectural plans, structural drawings, and final selections — all before breaking ground.

---

## Phase 2: Permits and Site Prep (4–8 Weeks)

After plans are approved, your builder pulls permits through the City of Wichita or the applicable county. This takes time — sometimes longer than expected depending on the municipality and permit load.

Meanwhile, site prep begins: clearing the lot, grading, and digging the foundation. In south-central Kansas, most homes go on a slab or a full basement, and your builder will guide you based on your lot and preferences.

---

## Phase 3: Framing and Rough-In (2–4 Months)

This is the phase where it starts to feel real. Framing goes up fast and you'll finally be able to walk through your future floor plan. After framing comes:

- **Rough electrical and plumbing** — everything that lives inside the walls
- **HVAC rough-in** — ductwork, returns, and mechanical placement
- **Insulation** — especially important in a Kansas climate with extreme summer heat and winter cold

Inspections happen at key milestones throughout this phase. Don't skip them, and make sure your builder isn't either.

---

## Phase 4: Drywall, Finishes, and Fixtures (2–3 Months)

Once inspections are cleared, the finish work begins. Drywall goes up, paint goes on, and then comes the part most homeowners actually enjoy: making it look like home.

Cabinets, countertops, flooring, tile, lighting, plumbing fixtures — this is where your selections from months ago get installed. Expect delays on custom-order items. Lead times on cabinets, windows, and specialty fixtures have been unpredictable in recent years, so order early.

### Pro Tip
Walk the house with your builder regularly during this phase. It's much easier to fix a light placement or adjust a cabinet height before it's done than after.

---

## Phase 5: Final Inspections and Punch List (2–4 Weeks)

Before you close and get your keys, there's a final inspection by the city and a punch list walk-through with your builder. This is where you document anything that needs to be corrected — paint touch-ups, trim gaps, door adjustments, etc.

A reputable builder takes the punch list seriously and gets it done before closing. Be thorough. This is your last chance to address items under the original contract scope.

---

## The Total Timeline

From the day you sign a contract to the day you move in, expect **10–16 months** for a typical custom home in the Wichita area. Simpler designs and quick decision-making can compress that. High-end custom builds with complex finishes can stretch it.
