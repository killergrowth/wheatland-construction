## What Does a Kitchen Remodel Actually Cost in 2026?

If you've started googling kitchen remodel costs, you've probably noticed the range is enormous — anywhere from $10,000 to $100,000-plus depending on who you ask. That gap isn't just noise. It reflects the real difference between a basic refresh and a full gut-and-rebuild. Here's what that actually looks like on the ground in Wichita, KS.

At Wheatland Construction, we've done enough kitchens in the Wichita area to give you straight numbers — not national averages padded to look good in a magazine.

---

## Average Kitchen Remodel Cost by Project Scope

### Minor Remodel: $10,000 – $25,000

A minor remodel typically means updating the surface-level stuff: new cabinet doors or paint, updated hardware, fresh countertops, and modern fixtures. You're keeping the existing layout and major appliances. This is the entry point for homeowners who want a refresh without the disruption of a full demo.

**What's included:**
- Cabinet refacing or painting
- New countertops (laminate, entry-level quartz)
- Faucet and fixture upgrades
- New flooring (LVP or tile)
- Basic lighting updates

### Mid-Range Remodel: $25,000 – $60,000

This is where most Wichita homeowners land when they want a real upgrade. You're replacing cabinets, upgrading to quartz or stone countertops, pulling in new appliances, and often reconfiguring the layout slightly for better flow. Electrical and plumbing work usually enters the picture here.

**What's included:**
- Semi-custom cabinetry
- Quartz or granite countertops
- New appliance package
- Updated electrical (outlets, under-cabinet lighting)
- Tile backsplash
- Possible layout adjustments

### Major Remodel: $60,000 – $100,000+

A major kitchen remodel means you're going all-in — structural changes, full custom cabinetry, high-end appliances, expanded square footage, or a complete layout redesign. This level of project requires a licensed general contractor who can coordinate plumbing, electrical, HVAC, and structural work under one roof.

**What's included:**
- Custom cabinetry and built-ins
- Premium countertops (quartzite, leathered granite, waterfall edges)
- High-end appliance packages (Sub-Zero, Wolf, etc.)
- Island additions or expansions
- Structural changes (wall removal, added square footage)
- Custom tile work and specialty finishes

## What Drives Kitchen Remodel Costs in Wichita

### 1. Layout Changes
Moving plumbing is one of the fastest ways to add cost. If your new design keeps the sink and dishwasher in the same location, you save. If you're moving the sink across the room or adding a second prep sink, expect to add $2,000–$8,000 just for the rough-in work.

### 2. Cabinet Quality
Cabinets typically account for 30–40% of your total kitchen remodel budget. Stock cabinets are the most affordable but limit your options. Semi-custom gives you better sizing and finish choices. Full custom is built to your exact specs and comes at a premium — but in an older Wichita home with non-standard dimensions, it's often worth it.

### 3. Countertop Material
Laminate starts around $20–$40 per square foot installed. Quartz runs $70–$120. Natural stone, quartzite, or specialty materials can push $150+ per square foot. In a standard 30-square-foot countertop run, that difference adds up fast.

### 4. Appliances
A basic appliance package (range, hood, dishwasher, refrigerator) can be done for $3,000–$5,000. A professional-grade package from a premium brand can easily hit $15,000–$25,000 or more.

### 5. Labor and Permitting
Labor costs in Wichita are generally lower than coastal markets, but they've risen with material costs and trade demand. Plan for 20–30% of your total budget going to labor. Permits for kitchen remodels in Wichita typically run $150–$500 depending on scope, and any licensed GC will pull them — skip anyone who suggests skipping permits.

## How Long Does a Kitchen Remodel Take?

- **Minor remodel:** 2–4 weeks
- **Mid-range remodel:** 6–10 weeks
- **Major remodel:** 3–5 months

Lead times on custom cabinetry are often the longest variable — sometimes 8–12 weeks out from order. Plan your project start date around that, not around demolition day.
