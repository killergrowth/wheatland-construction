If you're a homeowner in Wichita, chances are you've already got an asphalt shingle roof — or you're about to get one. They cover the overwhelming majority of homes in our area, and there's a good reason for that: asphalt shingles strike the best balance of cost, durability, and style for Kansas weather. But "asphalt shingle roof" covers a lot of ground, and the choices you make matter more than most homeowners realize until something goes wrong.

Here's what you actually need to know before your next roof goes on — or before you decide whether your current one needs to come off.

## Why Asphalt Shingles Dominate in Wichita

Wichita throws a lot at a roof. Hail in the spring, 100-degree heat in the summer, high winds pretty much year-round, and the occasional ice storm in winter. Asphalt shingles handle that range of conditions better than almost any other roofing material at a comparable price point.

There are two main types you'll run into:

- **3-tab shingles** — flat, uniform, budget-friendly. Still used, but increasingly rare on new installs because they don't hold up as well to wind and hail.
- **Architectural (dimensional) shingles** — thicker, layered construction that mimics the look of wood shake or slate, with significantly better wind and impact resistance. This is what most Wichita roofers install today, and for good reason.

If you're pricing out a new roof, expect architectural shingles to be the default recommendation — and expect them to cost a bit more upfront in exchange for a roof that actually survives the next hailstorm.

## What Actually Drives the Cost

Search for "asphalt shingle roof" and you'll find cost calculators all over the place, but most of them are generic and don't account for local labor rates, local code requirements, or the condition of your existing roof deck. A few things that move the number more than people expect:

**Roof deck condition.** If there's rot or damage under your current shingles, that needs to be replaced before new shingles go down. You won't know this for certain until the old roof comes off — which is why a flat quote over the phone should always be treated as an estimate, not a final number.

**Pitch and complexity.** A steep roof or one with a lot of valleys, dormers, and penetrations takes longer and costs more in labor than a simple gable roof of the same square footage.

**Tear-off vs. layover.** Kansas allows up to two layers of shingles in some cases, but a full tear-off is almost always the better long-term call. It lets your contractor inspect the deck, fix any issues, and gives the new shingles a clean, flat surface to seal against.

**Shingle quality and warranty.** Builder-grade shingles and premium architectural shingles with a 30-year or lifetime warranty can differ by a meaningful amount per square — and the difference shows up in how the roof performs after year 10.

## Hail Damage Is the Elephant in the Room

If you own a home in Wichita, you've probably already filed a hail claim at some point, or you know someone who has. Asphalt shingles take the brunt of hail impact, and the damage isn't always obvious from the ground. Granule loss, bruising, and cracked mat fibers can all be present on a roof that still "looks fine" from the driveway.

This matters for two reasons. First, hail damage that goes unaddressed tends to turn into a leak eventually — it's rarely a question of if, just when. Second, insurance companies pay close attention to timelines after a storm, so getting an inspection sooner rather than later protects your claim.

If it's been a year or two since the last major hailstorm and you haven't had your roof looked at, that's worth putting on the calendar.

## Lifespan: What's Realistic in Our Climate

Manufacturers love to advertise 30-year or even lifetime warranties on asphalt shingles, but real-world lifespan in Wichita tends to run shorter than the marketing numbers because of our weather swings. A well-installed architectural shingle roof here typically holds up for 20-25 years before a full replacement makes sense, assuming no major hail event forces the issue sooner.

Ventilation plays a bigger role in that number than most homeowners expect. A roof deck that can't breathe properly traps heat and moisture, which cooks the shingles from underneath and shortens their life — sometimes by years. If your attic feels like an oven in July, that's not just a comfort problem; it's a roof-life problem.

## What to Ask Before You Sign a Contract

A few questions separate a solid roofing contractor from one that'll leave you with problems down the road:

- Will this be a full tear-off, and will you inspect the deck before re-decking or re-shingling?
- What ventilation changes, if any, do you recommend for this roof?
- What's the manufacturer's warranty, and is it backed by a workmanship warranty from your crew?
- Are you using manufacturer-certified installation methods (this can affect whether the warranty is even valid)?

If a contractor can't answer these clearly, that's a signal to keep looking.

## Getting It Right the First Time

An asphalt shingle roof is one of the biggest-ticket maintenance items on a Wichita home, and it's not something you want to redo in five years because corners got cut the first time. The right pitch, the right ventilation, the right shingle grade, and a clean tear-off make the difference between a roof that lasts two decades and one that starts causing problems by year eight.
