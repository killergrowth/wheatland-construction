## How Long Does a Roof Last? Here's What You Need to Know

If you're a homeowner in Wichita, KS, chances are you've looked up at your roof and wondered: *how much longer does this thing have?* It's a fair question — your roof is one of the most critical (and expensive) components of your home. Knowing what to expect from your roof's lifespan can help you plan ahead, avoid emergency repairs, and protect your investment.

Here's a straightforward breakdown of roof lifespans by material, the factors that affect longevity in Kansas, and the warning signs that mean it's time to call a contractor.

---

## Roof Lifespan by Material

Not all roofs are created equal. The material your roof is made from is the single biggest factor in how long it will last.

### Asphalt Shingles (Most Common)
The majority of homes in Wichita use asphalt shingles. Here's what you can expect:

- **3-tab shingles:** 15–20 years
- **Architectural (dimensional) shingles:** 25–30 years
- **Premium/designer shingles:** 30–40 years

Asphalt is affordable and widely available, but it's also the most susceptible to Kansas weather — particularly hail, heat, and freeze-thaw cycles.

### Metal Roofing
Metal roofs are becoming increasingly popular for good reason. A properly installed metal roof can last **40–70 years**, with minimal maintenance. They hold up well against hail and high winds — both of which are regular visitors to Wichita.

### Wood Shake
Cedar shake roofs offer a distinctive look and typically last **20–30 years** with proper maintenance. They require more upkeep than other materials and can be prone to moss and mildew in humid conditions.

### Tile and Slate
Clay tile and natural slate are the heavy hitters — slate roofs can last **75–100+ years** when installed correctly. They're rare in Wichita due to cost and structural requirements, but they're the longest-lasting option on the market.

---

## What Affects Roof Lifespan in Wichita, KS?

Kansas weather is no joke. Here's what shortens a roof's life faster than anything else in our area:

**Hail Storms**
Wichita sits in prime hail territory. Even "minor" hailstorms can cause granule loss on asphalt shingles, cracking, and hidden damage that accelerates aging by years. If you haven't had a post-storm inspection in the last few years, you're overdue.

**Wind**
Sustained winds and gusts from severe thunderstorms can lift shingles, compromise flashing, and create entry points for moisture.

**Heat and UV Exposure**
Kansas summers are brutal. Prolonged UV exposure dries out roofing materials, causes shingles to curl or crack, and breaks down sealants over time.

**Poor Installation or Ventilation**
A roof that's improperly installed — or one with inadequate attic ventilation — will fail years ahead of schedule. Heat buildup in the attic degrades shingles from the inside out.

**Maintenance (or Lack of It)**
Gutters packed with debris, moss growing along the edges, and ignored small leaks all quietly shorten your roof's effective life.

## Warning Signs Your Roof Is Near the End

You don't need to climb up there yourself. Here's what to watch for from the ground — and inside your home:

- **Missing or curling shingles** — a clear sign of age or wind damage
- **Granules in the gutters** — asphalt shingles shed granules as they deteriorate
- **Dark streaks or moss growth** — moisture retention and algae
- **Daylight through the roof boards** — visible in your attic
- **Water stains on ceilings or walls** — active or recent leaks
- **Sagging roof deck** — structural concern, needs immediate attention
- **Your roof is over 20 years old** — even if it looks okay, it may be living on borrowed time

## So… When Should You Replace Your Roof?

If your asphalt shingle roof is approaching 20 years, it's worth getting a professional inspection — even if you're not seeing obvious problems. Many roofs look fine from the curb but have underlying damage that becomes a serious (and costly) issue during the next storm season.

In Wichita, we see a lot of insurance-covered replacements following hail events. If it's been a few years since your last major storm and you haven't had an inspection, there's a real chance you have damage you don't know about.
