## How Do You Know When It's Time for a New Roof?

Most Wichita homeowners don't think about their roof until something goes wrong — and by then, "something wrong" has usually turned into water damage, mold, or a five-figure repair bill. The good news? Your roof almost always gives you warning signs before it fails completely. You just have to know what to look for.

Here's a breakdown of the most common signs you need a new roof, and why you shouldn't wait on any of them.

## Your Shingles Are Curling, Cracking, or Missing

Shingles are your roof's first line of defense. When they start to curl at the edges, crack across the surface, or go missing entirely after a storm, that's not cosmetic — that's structural. Wichita's weather puts serious wear on roofing materials. Hot summers, freeze-thaw cycles in winter, and the occasional hailstorm accelerate shingle deterioration faster than you might expect.

If you're seeing bald patches, curled corners, or shingles in your yard after a windstorm, it's time to get eyes on that roof.

## Granules Are Collecting in Your Gutters

Asphalt shingles are coated in granules that protect against UV and impact. When those granules start shedding — and you'll find them piling up in your gutters or downspout runoff — it means the shingles are past their prime. A little granule loss is normal on older roofs. Heavy, consistent loss means the shingles are wearing out from the inside and losing their ability to protect your home.

## Your Roof Is 20+ Years Old

The average asphalt shingle roof lasts 20–25 years. If yours is pushing that range and you haven't had it inspected recently, now's the time. Age alone doesn't mean replacement is imminent, but it does mean your roof is living on borrowed time — especially if it's gone through multiple Kansas storm seasons without a full inspection.

## You're Seeing Daylight in Your Attic

This one's a red flag. If you go into your attic during the day and see light coming through the roof boards, water and outside air are coming through the same gaps. While you're up there, check for water staining, dark spots, or streaks on the decking and rafters. Any of those indicate active or past moisture intrusion — a sign the roof has been compromised for a while.

## Your Energy Bills Are Climbing

A failing roof doesn't just let water in — it lets conditioned air out. If your heating and cooling bills have crept up without a clear reason, poor roof ventilation or insulation gaps from a deteriorating roof deck could be the culprit. Wichita summers are brutal enough without your HVAC system fighting a leaky roof.

## There's Sagging in the Roofline

A roof should be flat and straight along its ridge line. If you're noticing any sagging, dipping, or soft spots — especially near the edges or in the valleys — that's structural damage. It could mean the decking beneath the shingles has been saturated and is rotting, or that the underlying structure has shifted. This is one sign that needs immediate attention, not a "we'll get to it eventually" response.

## You've Had Multiple Repairs on the Same Spots

If you've called a roofer out to patch the same section two or three times, you're spending repair money on a roof that's giving up. Repeated failures in the same area typically mean the underlying issue — decking damage, flashing failure, or chronic leak points — isn't being solved by surface patches. At some point, continuing to repair is more expensive than replacing.
