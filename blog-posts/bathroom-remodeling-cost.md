## How Much Does a Bathroom Remodel Cost in Wichita, KS?

A bathroom remodel in Wichita typically runs **$8,000 to $25,000**, depending on the size of the space and how much you're changing. A simple refresh — new vanity, toilet, flooring, and paint — lands on the lower end, usually $8,000 to $12,000. A full gut remodel with a new tub-to-shower conversion, custom tile, and updated plumbing and electrical can push past $20,000, especially in older Wichita homes where outdated pipes or wiring need to be brought up to code before the pretty stuff goes in.

The honest answer is: it depends on what's behind your walls as much as what you want in front of them. Here's what actually drives that number up or down.

### What Drives Bathroom Remodeling Cost the Most

Three things move the needle more than anything else: scope, materials, and what's hiding behind your existing walls.

**Scope** is the biggest factor. Swapping a vanity and re-tiling a floor is a different job than moving plumbing to reconfigure the whole layout. Moving a toilet or shower drain means cutting into the slab or subfloor, which adds labor cost fast — often $1,500 to $3,000 just for the relocation work before any finishes go in.

**Materials** are where budgets swing the most. A builder-grade vanity and standard porcelain tile cost a fraction of a custom floating vanity with quartz counters and large-format tile. Shower glass is another big spread — a basic shower curtain rod costs almost nothing, while a frameless glass enclosure can run $1,200 to $2,500 installed.

**What's behind the walls** is the wildcard, and it's the one homeowners underestimate most. Once a contractor opens up a wall or floor in an older home, surprises show up: galvanized pipe that needs replacing, subfloor rot from a slow leak nobody noticed, or knob-and-tube wiring that isn't up to code. We build contingency into every quote for exactly this reason.

### Why Wichita Bathroom Remodels Cost What They Do

A lot of Wichita's housing stock was built between the 1950s and 1980s, and that era of construction shows up directly in remodel costs. Homes from that period often still have original galvanized plumbing or early copper lines that weren't sized for modern fixtures like rain showerheads or double vanities. When we open up a bathroom wall in a College Hill or Riverside bungalow, it's common to find plumbing that needs a partial re-pipe before new fixtures can even go in — that's an added cost that newer-build neighborhoods in Andover or Maize typically don't run into as often.

Kansas's weather swings matter too. Freeze-thaw cycles over 50-plus years can stress old pipe joints and slab foundations, which is part of why we always recommend a plumbing inspection before finalizing scope on any home built before 1985. It's a lot cheaper to find that issue during the estimate than after demo day.

### Typical Bathroom Remodel Cost Ranges by Project Type

Here's a general breakdown of what Wichita-area homeowners can expect, based on project scope:

- **Cosmetic refresh** ($8,000–$12,000): New vanity, toilet, flooring, paint, and fixtures. No layout changes, no plumbing moves.
- **Mid-range remodel** ($12,000–$18,000): New tile shower or tub surround, updated lighting and ventilation, possible minor layout tweaks.
- **Full gut remodel** ($18,000–$25,000+): Complete demo to studs, layout changes, plumbing relocation, custom tile work, and higher-end fixtures.
- **Primary suite bathroom addition or major expansion**: Often $25,000 and up, especially if square footage is added. This overlaps with our [home addition services](/additions/) for homeowners expanding their primary suite.

These ranges assume a standard 5x8 to 8x10 bathroom footprint. Larger primary bathrooms or ones requiring structural changes will run higher.

### How to Keep Your Remodel Within Budget

The single best way to control cost is locking in scope before demo starts — change orders mid-project are where budgets run away. Get a detailed written quote that separates labor, materials, and any plumbing or electrical contingency, so you know exactly what's fixed and what could shift if surprises come up.

It also helps to decide early which finishes matter most to you and where you're willing to go builder-grade. Spending more on a quality shower pan and waterproofing system (the stuff you'll never see again) and less on decorative tile accents is usually a smarter trade than the reverse — a leak behind the wall costs far more to fix later than it would have cost to do right the first time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to remodel a small bathroom?**
A small bathroom (5x8 or similar) typically costs $8,000 to $15,000 for a cosmetic-to-mid-range remodel, depending on whether you're changing the layout or just updating finishes.

**What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?**
Plumbing relocation and custom tile work are typically the biggest cost drivers. Moving a toilet or shower drain, or using large-format and handmade tile, adds labor hours that standard finishes don't require.

**Is it cheaper to remodel a bathroom or add a new one?**
Remodeling an existing bathroom is almost always cheaper than adding a new one, since adding a bathroom requires new plumbing lines, venting, and often structural work. A remodel reuses existing infrastructure.

**How long does a bathroom remodel take?**
A cosmetic refresh usually takes 1 to 2 weeks. A full gut remodel with plumbing changes typically takes 3 to 5 weeks, depending on material lead times and whether unexpected issues are found once walls are opened.

**Should I remodel my bathroom before selling my house?**
A moderate update (new vanity, updated lighting, fresh tile) often has strong return on investment for resale. A full luxury remodel rarely recoups its full cost at resale unless your home is in a higher price bracket already.

**Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Wichita?**
Most bathroom remodels involving plumbing or electrical changes require a permit in Wichita. Cosmetic-only updates (paint, fixtures, flooring with no plumbing moves) generally don't. A licensed contractor will pull the right permits as part of the project.

**Can I remodel a bathroom in stages to spread out the cost?**
Yes, though it's more efficient to at least complete demo and rough-in plumbing/electrical in one phase, then phase the finish work if budget requires it. Doing demo twice adds unnecessary labor cost.
