## What You Need to Know Before Hiring a Roofing Company

Your roof is one of the most critical components of your home or commercial building. When it fails, everything inside is at risk. Whether you're dealing with hail damage after a Kansas storm, a slow leak that's gone too long, or you're simply ready for a full replacement — choosing the right roofing company makes all the difference.

This guide breaks down exactly what to look for, what to avoid, and how to make a confident decision when hiring roofing companies in Wichita, KS.

--

## Types of Roofing Companies

Not every roofer handles every job. Understanding the different types helps you find the right fit:

### Residential Roofing Contractors

These companies specialize in single-family homes, townhomes, and smaller multi-family properties. They typically work with asphalt shingles, metal roofing, and flat roof systems on residential structures.

### Commercial Roofing Contractors

Commercial roofers handle larger, flat or low-slope roof systems — TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen — common on warehouses, office buildings, and retail centers. The materials, equipment, and experience required are significantly different from residential work.

### General Contractors with Roofing Services

Some general contractors offer roofing as part of a broader scope. This can be an advantage if you're dealing with storm damage that affected more than just the roof — siding, gutters, interior repairs — because one contractor can manage the full project.

## What to Look for in Roofing Companies in Wichita, KS

Wichita gets hit hard by hail, high winds, and severe thunderstorms. That makes roofing contractor selection especially important here. Here's what matters:

### 1. Local Experience

A company that's been operating in Wichita understands the regional climate, the common damage patterns, and the insurance claim process that follows a major storm. Out-of-state "storm chasers" show up after hail events and disappear before warranty issues surface. Avoid them.

### 2. Proper Licensing and Insurance

Any roofing contractor working in Kansas should carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation. Ask for certificates before work starts — not after. An uninsured crew on your roof means you're liable if someone gets hurt.

### 3. Manufacturer Certifications

Top-tier roofing companies earn certifications from manufacturers like GAF, Owens Corning, or CertainTeed. These certifications aren't just marketing — they mean the contractor has met installation standards that allow them to offer enhanced warranty coverage.

### 4. Written Estimates and Contracts

Get everything in writing. A reputable company will provide a detailed scope of work, material specifications, timeline, and payment terms before a single nail is driven. Vague verbal agreements are a red flag.

### 5. References and Reviews

Check Google, Yelp, and the BBB. Look for patterns — not just the star rating. A company with 80 reviews averaging 4.7 stars tells a better story than one with three five-star reviews posted in the same week.

## Red Flags to Watch Out For

- Requiring full payment upfront before work begins

- No physical local address or business presence

- High-pressure sales tactics pushing you to sign same-day

- Offering to waive your insurance deductible (this is insurance fraud)

- No written warranty on labor or materials

--

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign

Before committing to any roofing company, ask these directly:

- **Are you licensed and insured in Kansas?**

- **Who will actually be on my roof — your employees or subcontractors?**

- **What warranty do you offer on labor, and what does the manufacturer warranty cover?**

- **How do you handle unexpected damage found during the project?**

- **Do you pull permits when required?**

Good contractors answer these questions without hesitation. If you get pushback, keep looking.

## The Roofing Process: What to Expect

A standard residential roof replacement typically follows this sequence:

- **Inspection and estimate** — The contractor inspects your roof and provides a written quote.

- **Material selection** — You choose shingle style, color, and grade.

- **Permit pulled** (if required by your municipality)

- **Old roof stripped** — Existing materials removed down to the decking.

- **Deck inspection and repair** — Damaged sheathing is replaced before new materials go on.

- **New roof installed** — Underlayment, flashing, shingles, and ridge cap.

- **Cleanup and final walkthrough** — A reputable crew leaves your property cleaner than they found it.

Most residential replacements are completed in one to two days.
