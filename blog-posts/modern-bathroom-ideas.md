If you've scrolled Pinterest or walked through a few open houses lately, you've probably noticed bathrooms don't look like they used to. Builder-grade oak vanities and shiny brass fixtures have given way to cleaner lines, moodier palettes, and materials that feel more like a boutique hotel than a 1990s spec home. For homeowners across Wichita, Andover, Derby, and the surrounding metro, that shift means a lot of remodel conversations start the same way: "I want something modern, but I'm not totally sure what that means for my house."

Fair question. "Modern" gets thrown around loosely in bathroom design, so let's break down what's actually trending right now, what holds up long-term, and what to think about before you start tearing out tile.

## What "Modern" Actually Looks Like Right Now

Modern bathroom design in 2026 leans toward simplicity with texture. Think fewer busy patterns, more intentional material choices. A few things we're seeing requested most often on jobs around the Wichita area:

**Floating vanities.** Wall-mounted vanities create visual space in smaller bathrooms, which matters a lot in older Wichita homes where bathroom footprints weren't exactly generous. They also make cleaning the floor easier, which homeowners always appreciate once they actually live with one.

**Large-format tile.** Fewer grout lines, cleaner look, less maintenance. This applies to both floors and shower walls, and it's one of the biggest visual differences between an older bathroom and a newly remodeled one.

**Matte black and brushed fixtures.** Chrome hasn't disappeared, but matte black, brushed nickel, and warm brass are dominating faucet and hardware choices. They pair well with both cooler and warmer color palettes, which gives homeowners more flexibility down the road.

**Curbless or low-profile showers.** Walk-in showers without a raised lip aren't just an aesthetic choice — they're increasingly requested for accessibility and resale value. If you're planning to stay in your home long-term, this is worth a real conversation during the [bathroom remodeling](/bathroom-remodeling/wichita-ks/) planning phase.

**Warmer neutral palettes.** Stark white and gray are softening into warmer whites, greiges, and the occasional deep accent wall or vanity color. All-white bathrooms photograph well but can feel cold in person, especially during Kansas winters.

## What Doesn't Hold Up Long-Term

Not every trend deserves a spot in your bathroom. A few things worth skipping even if they're everywhere online right now:

- **Overly trendy tile patterns.** Herringbone and fish-scale tile look great today, but highly specific patterns can date a bathroom faster than a safer, more classic layout.
- **All-natural stone without sealing plans.** Marble looks stunning in photos, but it requires real maintenance. If nobody's told you about sealing schedules, that's a red flag.
- **Oversized soaking tubs nobody uses.** A lot of homeowners request a statement tub, then realize a few years later they almost never use it and it's eating floor space a bigger shower could've used instead.

## What This Means for Older Wichita Homes

A lot of the homes we work on around Wichita, Andover, and Derby were built decades before "spa-like bathroom" was a design phrase anyone used. That creates a few practical considerations modern design inspiration doesn't always account for:

**Plumbing location matters.** Moving a toilet or shower drain even a couple feet can significantly change the cost of a remodel. Floating vanities and curbless showers both sometimes require plumbing adjustments that aren't obvious from a Pinterest board.

**Smaller footprints need smarter layouts.** Many older Wichita bathrooms run 5x8 feet or smaller. Modern design doesn't require a bigger room — it requires a layout that doesn't waste the space you have. This is where working with someone local who's actually remodeled in these housing stock types pays off, versus trying to replicate a magazine layout built for a much larger room.

**Ventilation and moisture control matter more with certain materials.** Large-format tile and matte finishes still need proper exhaust fans sized to the room. Skipping this step causes problems with grout, finishes, and even drywall down the road — something easy to overlook when you're focused on finishes instead of mechanicals.

## Budgeting for a Modern Bathroom Remodel

Costs vary a lot depending on scope, but a few general patterns hold true:

- Fixture and hardware upgrades (faucets, lighting, mirrors) are the lowest-cost way to modernize a bathroom without touching layout.
- Tile and vanity replacement without moving plumbing sits in the middle range.
- Full layout changes — moving a shower, relocating a toilet, or converting a tub to a curbless shower — cost more because of the plumbing and sometimes electrical work involved.

If you're planning a full [major remodel](/major-remodels/wichita-ks/) rather than a surface-level update, it's worth having that conversation early so the budget and the design vision actually line up.
