When you’re planning a custom home or major remodel, one of the biggest questions is: *How much will it cost?* While some contractors lock clients into a lump-sum contract, Wheatland Construction uses a **cost-plus contract**—a transparent approach that keeps your project flexible, honest, and in your best interest.

If you’ve never heard of a cost-plus contract, let’s break down what it is, why it matters, and how it benefits you as a homeowner.

## What Is a Cost-Plus Contract?

A cost-plus contract is exactly what it sounds like:

- **Cost** – You pay the actual costs of materials, labor, and subcontractors.

- **Plus** – A clearly defined percentage or fee that covers the contractor’s overhead and profit.

Instead of locking you into a single “guess-timated” price at the start, a cost-plus contract shows you **exactly what your money is paying for, line by line.**

## The Advantages of Cost-Plus Pricing

Homeowners often find this model fairer and more transparent than a lump-sum contract. Here’s why:

### 1. Full Transparency

You see where every dollar goes—from lumber and roofing materials to subcontractor bids. There are no hidden markups or surprises.

### 2. Flexibility for Changes

Dream homes evolve during the design/build process. With cost-plus, you can make changes or upgrades without having to renegotiate an entire contract.

### 3. True Cost Control

Because you’re paying the actual costs, you’re never stuck overpaying for inflated estimates. If materials come in lower than expected, you benefit.

### 4. Higher Quality Results

Some lump-sum contracts incentivize cutting corners to protect profit margins. With cost-plus, your builder is motivated to focus on craftsmanship, not cost-cutting.

### 5. Trust and Partnership

Cost-plus aligns the homeowner and builder as a team working toward the same goal: delivering your project on budget, with character and integrity.

## Cost-Plus vs. Fixed-Price: Which Is Better?

**Fixed-price contracts** may seem simpler, but they often come with hidden risks:

- Inflated bids to cover “what-if” scenarios.

- Lower-quality materials or labor to protect profit.

- Tension when homeowners request changes mid-project.

With **cost-plus**, you avoid those pitfalls. Instead, you get clarity, choice, and a builder whose interests are aligned with yours.
