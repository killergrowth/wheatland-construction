If you've started researching a kitchen remodel in Wichita, you've probably noticed the same thing we hear from homeowners every week: the estimates are all over the map, and nobody seems to agree on what a "normal" project actually costs or takes. That's not an accident — kitchen remodels vary more than almost any other home improvement project because so much depends on layout changes, material choices, and what's hiding behind your walls once demo starts.

Here's what we want every Wichita homeowner to understand before they get a single quote.

## What Actually Drives Kitchen Remodel Costs

Most people assume cabinets and countertops are the biggest line item. Sometimes they are — but just as often, the real cost driver is something invisible at the planning stage: electrical panel capacity, plumbing relocation, or load-bearing walls standing between you and that open-concept layout you saw on Pinterest.

A few things that consistently move the budget more than homeowners expect:

- **Moving plumbing or gas lines.** Shifting a sink or range even a few feet can mean rerouting lines under the slab or through a crawlspace, which adds real labor hours.
- **Structural changes.** Removing a wall between the kitchen and dining room almost always means adding a beam, and that means an engineer, permits, and inspection — not just a weekend demo job.
- **Electrical upgrades.** Older Wichita homes, especially in neighborhoods built before the 1980s, often need panel upgrades to support modern appliances, under-cabinet lighting, and additional outlets required by current code.
- **Cabinet configuration vs. material.** Custom cabinet boxes and modified layouts cost more than finish upgrades on stock cabinet shells. Swapping door style and hardware is cheap; changing the footprint is not.

If you want a realistic number before you ever talk to a contractor, start by being honest about whether you're changing the layout or just refreshing what's already there. Cosmetic refreshes (new countertops, backsplash, paint, hardware) and full layout remodels are essentially two different projects with two different price tiers.

## Timeline: What to Actually Expect

Most full kitchen remodels in our area run **6 to 10 weeks** from demo to final walkthrough, assuming no major surprises and materials arrive on schedule. Cosmetic-only updates can move faster, often 2 to 4 weeks.

The biggest timeline killer isn't labor — it's material lead times. Custom cabinets alone can take 6-8 weeks to arrive after you place the order, which is why we always recommend locking in cabinet selections early, even before demo starts. Countertop templating also has to happen after cabinets are installed, which adds another week or two for fabrication and install. Plan backward from your target completion date, not forward from demo day.

## Permits and Inspections in Wichita

Any kitchen remodel involving electrical, plumbing, or structural work requires permits through the City of Wichita (or your local municipality if you're in [Andover](/custom-home-building/andover-ks/), [Derby](/custom-home-building/derby-ks/), or another surrounding city). This isn't optional paperwork — skipping permits can create real problems when you sell the house, since unpermitted work often has to be disclosed and can scare off buyers or complicate appraisals.

A licensed general contractor should be pulling these permits as part of the project, not leaving it to you to figure out. If a contractor tells you permits "aren't necessary" for electrical or plumbing work, that's a red flag worth paying attention to.

## Should You Remodel or Rebuild?

For homeowners dealing with a kitchen that needs more than a facelift — outdated layout, insufficient square footage, or structural issues — it's worth asking whether a [home addition](/home-additions/) that expands the kitchen footprint makes more sense than squeezing a full remodel into the existing walls. We've had several Wichita clients realize that bumping out 4-6 feet into the backyard solved layout problems that no amount of clever cabinet design could fix.

On the other end, if your bones are solid and you're mainly tired of the look, a focused update — new countertops, a tile backsplash, refinished or repainted cabinets, updated lighting — can deliver most of the visual impact at a fraction of the cost and disruption of a full gut remodel.
