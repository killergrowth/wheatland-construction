## What Is a Bump Out Addition?

A bump out addition is a small-scale home extension — typically 2 to 15 feet deep — that adds square footage to one room without the cost of a full addition. Common bump outs expand kitchens, primary bedrooms, bathrooms, or living areas. They're one of the most cost-effective ways to gain usable space in your home without moving.

In Wichita, bump outs have become a popular alternative to full additions for homeowners who want more room but don't want to blow the budget or move through the chaos of a major build.

---

## Bump Out Addition Cost in 2026: The Numbers

Nationally, bump out additions cost between **$5,000 and $30,000**, but the realistic range for most homeowners in Wichita, KS lands somewhere between **$8,000 and $22,000** depending on scope, finishes, and what trades need to be involved.

Here's a general cost breakdown:

### By Size
- **Small (2–4 ft, ~50 sq ft):** $5,000–$12,000
- **Medium (5–8 ft, ~100–150 sq ft):** $12,000–$20,000
- **Large (10–15 ft, ~200+ sq ft):** $20,000–$35,000+

### By Room Type
- **Kitchen bump out:** $10,000–$25,000 (plumbing and cabinetry drive costs)
- **Bathroom bump out:** $8,000–$18,000 (plumbing required)
- **Bedroom or living room:** $7,000–$15,000 (fewer mechanical trades)

### Per Square Foot
Most bump outs in the Wichita area run **$150–$250 per square foot** for a finished space. That's lower than a full addition, which often starts at $200–$300/sq ft or more.

---

## What Drives the Cost Up (or Down)

Several factors push bump out costs in either direction:

**Foundation type** — Cantilevered bump outs (no new foundation) cost significantly less than those requiring a crawl space or slab extension. If you can avoid touching the foundation, you save thousands.

**Mechanical involvement** — If plumbing, HVAC, or electrical needs to be extended into the new space, expect $2,000–$8,000 in additional trade costs depending on complexity.

**Exterior finish** — Matching your existing siding, roofline, and trim is a hidden cost many homeowners underestimate. Mismatched finishes drop resale value and look unfinished. Budget for a quality match.

**Permits and inspections** — In Wichita and Sedgwick County, bump outs over a certain size require a permit. A licensed contractor will pull this for you; budget $500–$1,500 for permits depending on scope.

**Interior finishes** — Hardwood floors, custom cabinetry, tile work — these choices can easily double the base cost of the structural work itself.

## Common Mistakes That Cost Wichita Homeowners More

1. **Going with the lowest bid.** Bump outs involve structural work. A contractor who underbids is either cutting corners or will hit you with change orders later.
2. **Skipping the permit.** Unpermitted work creates problems at resale and can get you forced to tear it out. Not worth it.
3. **Not planning for mechanical trades upfront.** Finding out mid-project that plumbing needs to be rerouted is expensive. A good contractor identifies this in the estimate phase.
4. **Underestimating finish costs.** The structural shell is the starting point — finishes are often 30–50% of total project cost.

## Is a Bump Out Worth It in Wichita?

In most cases, yes — especially if your alternative is moving. Wichita's housing market has tightened, and buying up into a larger home means higher mortgage rates on top of a higher purchase price. A well-executed bump out adds functional space and can return **50–75 cents on the dollar** at resale when done right.

The key word is *done right*. Framing, insulation, and exterior finish quality all affect whether the space feels like part of the house or an obvious afterthought.
