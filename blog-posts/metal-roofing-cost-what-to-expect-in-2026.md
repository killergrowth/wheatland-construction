## Metal Roofing Cost: What Wichita Homeowners Are Actually Paying in 2026

If you've been getting quotes for a new roof and metal keeps coming up, you're probably asking the same question everyone asks first: *how much does it actually cost?*

The honest answer is that metal roofing costs vary — but not randomly. There are specific factors that drive your price up or down, and once you understand them, the quotes you're getting will start to make a lot more sense.

Here's what homeowners in the Wichita, KS area should expect when budgeting for a metal roof in 2026.

--

## Average Metal Roofing Cost in 2026

Nationally, metal roofing runs between **$7 and $18 per square foot installed**, depending on the type of metal and complexity of the job. For a typical 1,500–2,000 sq ft home, that translates to roughly:

- **Standing seam metal roof:** $15,000–$35,000

- **Exposed fastener (corrugated/ribbed) panels:** $8,000–$20,000

- **Metal shingles or tiles:** $10,000–$25,000

In the **Wichita, KS market**, prices tend to land in the mid-range nationally. Labor costs here are generally lower than coastal markets, but material prices have climbed with post-pandemic supply chain adjustments. Most local homeowners are seeing complete metal roof installs come in between **$12,000 and $28,000** depending on roof size, pitch, and material choice.

## What Drives Metal Roofing Cost?

### 1. Material Type

This is the biggest variable. Your main options:

- **Steel (Galvalume or Galvanized):** Most common, most affordable. Galvalume is the standard for residential standing seam.

- **Aluminum:** Lighter and rust-resistant — great for coastal areas but less necessary in Kansas.

- **Copper or Zinc:** Premium options with 70–100 year lifespans. Beautiful, but expensive. Expect 3–5x the cost of steel.

### 2. Roof Profile

Standing seam (hidden fastener) costs more to install than exposed fastener panels because it's more labor-intensive and requires more precise fabrication. But standing seam also performs better long-term and looks more refined.

### 3. Roof Pitch and Complexity

Steep roofs, multiple valleys, skylights, dormers, and chimneys all add time and labor. A simple gable roof on a ranch home will almost always cost less per square foot than a complex hip roof with multiple penetrations.

### 4. Tear-Off and Decking

If your existing roof needs to come off (most installs do), add $1,000–$3,000 for removal and disposal. If the decking underneath is damaged, that adds more. A good contractor will assess this during the inspection.

### 5. Insulation and Underlayment

Metal roofs require proper underlayment — typically a high-temp synthetic or peel-and-stick membrane — to prevent condensation and noise. Some homeowners also add rigid insulation above the decking. These aren't optional; they're part of a quality install.

## Is Metal Roofing Worth the Higher Upfront Cost?

For most Wichita homeowners — yes, and here's why:

- **Lifespan:** Quality metal roofs last 40–70 years. Asphalt shingles average 15–25.

- **Wind resistance:** Metal roofs are rated for 120–160 mph winds. Kansas weather makes this matter.

- **Insurance savings:** Many carriers offer premium discounts for impact-resistant metal roofing.

- **Energy efficiency:** Reflective coatings reduce cooling loads — meaningful in a Wichita summer.

- **Low maintenance:** No curling, no granule loss, no annual patching.

When you factor in that you may replace an asphalt roof 2–3 times over the life of one metal roof, the cost gap narrows significantly.

--

## Red Flags to Watch for When Getting Quotes

- Quotes with no line-item breakdown

- Contractors who can't specify the gauge or profile of the metal

- No mention of underlayment or decking inspection

- No written warranty on labor (manufacturer warranties don't cover installation errors)

A reputable contractor will walk you through exactly what they're installing and why. If a quote feels vague, ask questions — or get another opinion.
