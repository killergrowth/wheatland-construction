## Major Remodel vs New Build: Which Is Right for Your Home?

If you've outgrown your current home or you're staring down a list of repairs that never seems to shrink, you've probably asked yourself the same question thousands of Wichita homeowners ask every year: do I remodel or just build new?

There's no universal answer. But there are clear signals that point one direction or the other — and knowing them before you commit can save you six figures and a whole lot of frustration.

Here's how to think through the major remodel vs new build decision the right way.

---

## What Counts as a "Major Remodel"?

A major remodel goes well beyond swapping out countertops or painting walls. We're talking about projects that change the bones of your home — additions, full kitchen gut-and-rebuilds, primary suite expansions, basement finishes, or reconfiguring load-bearing walls.

In Wichita, these projects typically run anywhere from $80,000 to $300,000+ depending on scope, existing structure condition, and materials. The advantage? You keep your lot, your neighborhood, your address — and often your mortgage rate.

---

## What Does Building New Actually Cost in Wichita?

New construction in the Wichita area averages roughly **$150–$250 per square foot** for a custom build, depending on finishes and lot prep. Add in land acquisition, utility hookups, permits, and landscaping, and a new build can easily land between $400,000 and $700,000 for a mid-size home.

That number surprises a lot of people. Building new isn't always cheaper — especially if you already own your lot and your existing structure is fundamentally sound.

---

## When a Major Remodel Makes More Sense

**Your location is locked in.** Wichita's best neighborhoods — College Hill, Eastborough, Riverside — don't have open lots. If you're already there, you can't rebuild your way back to that address.

**The bones are good.** Older Wichita homes built in the mid-20th century were often built with quality lumber and solid foundations. If the structure is sound, a remodel lets you keep what's working and upgrade what isn't.

**You want to stay in your school district.** This is one of the most common reasons Wichita families remodel rather than relocate. USD 259 attendance boundaries matter, and moving across town can flip that.

**Your equity is working for you.** Remodeling allows you to leverage existing home equity through HELOCs or renovation loans — often at better terms than a full construction loan.

---

## When Building New Makes More Sense

**The existing structure has serious problems.** Foundation issues, outdated electrical throughout, major plumbing problems, or extensive mold remediation can push remodel costs toward — or past — new build territory. At that point, you're paying twice for the same result.

**You want full control over the layout.** Remodels work within constraints. New construction doesn't. If your dream home has a layout that simply can't be achieved with your current footprint, building new gives you that blank slate.

**You have land.** If you've inherited property, own acreage outside of town, or found an infill lot in a Wichita neighborhood you love, new construction becomes a serious option worth pricing out.

**Long-term maintenance costs matter.** New builds come with warranties, new mechanicals, and modern energy efficiency. Older homes — even beautifully remodeled ones — can carry higher long-term maintenance costs.

---

## The Question Most People Skip

Before debating remodel vs new build, answer this first: **what's actually wrong with the house?**

Too many homeowners plan a full gut remodel when what they really need is a well-designed addition. Others price out new construction when a structural issue turns out to be straightforward to fix.

An experienced general contractor will give you a real assessment before you commit to either path. In Wichita, that conversation costs you nothing upfront — and it can redirect tens of thousands of dollars.
