If you've started shopping for kitchen cabinets, you've probably noticed something fast: the price range is enormous, the terminology is confusing, and every showroom seems to have a different opinion on what you "need." Stock, semi-custom, custom, refacing, RTA — it's a lot to sort through before you've even picked a wood species.

We build and remodel kitchens across Wichita and the surrounding area, and cabinets are consistently the part of the project homeowners have the most questions about — and the part where a wrong decision gets expensive fast. Here's what actually matters.

## Stock, Semi-Custom, or Custom — What's the Real Difference?

**Stock cabinets** come in preset sizes and a limited number of finishes. They're the most affordable option and can work well in a straightforward kitchen layout, but you're boxed into standard dimensions, which can leave awkward gaps or force compromises in your layout.

**Semi-custom cabinets** are stock boxes with more flexibility — different finishes, some size adjustments, more door styles. This is where most Wichita kitchen remodels land, because it balances cost against the ability to actually fit your space well.

**Custom cabinets** are built to your exact kitchen, inch by inch. They cost more and take longer to produce, but they're the right call for unusual layouts, high-end finishes, or kitchens where every inch of storage counts.

If your current kitchen has odd angles, a load-bearing column in a weird spot, or ceiling heights that aren't standard — which shows up more often than you'd think in older Wichita neighborhoods and some of the custom builds we see in Andover and Bel Aire — semi-custom or custom is usually worth the extra cost. Stock cabinets in a non-standard space almost always result in filler panels and wasted storage.

## Refacing vs. Full Replacement

Not every kitchen needs a full teardown. If your cabinet boxes are solid — no water damage, no structural issues, no warping — refacing can get you a dramatically different look for roughly a third to half the cost of full replacement. Refacing means keeping the existing boxes and replacing just the doors, drawer fronts, and hardware, often with a new veneer on exposed box faces.

The catch: refacing only works if the layout is already working for you. If you're trying to add counter space, change your storage configuration, reroute plumbing, or open up the kitchen to another room, you're looking at full replacement regardless of what the cabinet boxes look like.

We walk every client through this assessment before quoting anything, because recommending a full cabinet replacement when refacing would've solved the problem is how contractors lose trust — and recommending refacing on boxes that won't hold up is how homeowners end up redoing the job in three years.

## What Actually Drives Cost

Material is the obvious lever — solid wood costs more than engineered wood with a veneer, and certain species (hickory, cherry, quarter-sawn oak) run higher than others. But three other factors matter just as much and get overlooked constantly:

- **Door style complexity.** A flat slab door is cheaper to produce than a raised panel or shaker-style door with applied molding.
- **Box construction.** Plywood boxes hold up better over time than particleboard, especially in a humid Kansas summer where moisture matters more than people expect.
- **Hardware and soft-close mechanisms.** Soft-close hinges and drawer slides add cost per unit, but they're one of the few upgrades that pays off daily rather than just looking nice in photos.

A realistic full kitchen cabinet package for an average Wichita-area kitchen typically runs from the lower five figures for semi-custom work up through significantly more for full custom builds with premium finishes. Anyone quoting a number without having seen your kitchen, your layout, and your material preferences is guessing.

## Timeline: Plan for Longer Than You Think

This is the part that catches people off guard. Stock cabinets might be available in a couple of weeks. Semi-custom typically runs 4-8 weeks for manufacturing. Full custom cabinetry can take 8-12 weeks or more before it even arrives on site — and that's before installation, which adds its own timeline depending on scope.

If you're coordinating a full [kitchen remodeling](/bathroom-remodeling/wichita-ks/) project with a target move-in or event date, cabinet lead time should be one of the first things you lock down, not something you figure out after demo day.

## Questions Worth Asking Before You Sign

- Is this box construction plywood or particleboard?
- What's the actual lead time from order to delivery, not just "in stock"?
- Are soft-close hinges and slides included or an upgrade?
- What's the warranty on the boxes versus the finish?
- Who handles installation — is it included, or a separate contractor?

A straight answer to all five is a good sign you're working with someone who isn't just trying to close the sale.
